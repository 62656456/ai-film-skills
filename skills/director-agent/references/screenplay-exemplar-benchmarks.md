# Excellent Screenplay Calibration

Use real, published screenplays as calibration for complete story logic, character behavior, and dialogue. These are evidence sources, not templates. Do not copy their characters, plots, settings, scene order, or dialogue.

For a serious, complete, full, or explicitly excellent screenplay, read at least one causality benchmark and one dialogue benchmark relevant to the task. The two benchmarks may be different scenes from one screenplay. If a source cannot be opened, use another real published screenplay; do not pretend the source or its pages were read.

## Source And Copyright Boundary

This Skill does not bundle full copyrighted screenplays. It stores verified source editions, scene ranges, and transferable mechanisms. Read the actual source when needed, quote only within applicable limits, and use A3-20/A3-21 to move methods rather than expression.

Before claiming that a draft was calibrated, keep this internal receipt:

```text
real screenplay and edition identity
actual pages/scenes opened in this task
observable action/dialogue causality found in those pages
A3-20 mechanism extraction
A3-21 original transfer decision
recognizable plot/character/setting/dialogue similarities explicitly banned
surface-cluster check: whether two or more distinctive sensory, occupational, spatial, deadline, or power-shift elements still resemble the benchmark
```

Reading this benchmark file alone does not satisfy the receipt. If a surface cluster remains recognizable even without copied lines or plot, choose a different benchmark or redesign the carrier while preserving only the abstract mechanism. If no actual screenplay source can be opened, mark `EXEMPLAR CALIBRATION INCOMPLETE` internally and do not describe the draft as calibrated, excellent, or benchmark-passing. Keep the receipt out of a pure screenplay delivery unless the user asks for evidence.

### Parasite

- Authorship: screenplay by Bong Joon Ho and Han Jin Won; English FYC screenplay.
- Source identity: [NEON official film page](https://www.neonrated.com/film/parasite).
- Readable screenplay edition: [144-page FYC PDF mirror](https://script-pdf.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/parasite-script-pdf.pdf).
- Edition boundary: the English FYC text supports structure, action, and dialogue-mechanism study; do not treat its English wording as the original Korean character voice.

Calibration scenes:

- PDF pages 12-25: a job opportunity becomes a family infiltration only after the protagonist observes a new need and changes strategy. Test whether the current character uses information obtained in the scene rather than author knowledge.
- PDF pages 73-90: control changes hands through concrete evidence, exposure, and an eight-minute deadline. Test whether dialogue changes leverage and available actions rather than merely restating positions.
- PDF pages 125-131: prior plans, objects, social humiliation, spatial conditions, and character choices converge in the climax. Test whether the ending uses established conditions and still requires a costly human action.

Do not imitate the basement, class setting, stone, peach, smell, or family-infiltration plot. Transfer only the causal mechanism.

### Get Out

- Authorship: written by Jordan Peele; Universal awards-season draft.
- Source identity: [Universal official film page](https://www.universalpictures.com/movies/get-out).
- Readable screenplay edition: [100-page awards draft PDF mirror](https://assets.scriptslug.com/live/pdf/scripts/get-out-2017.pdf).
- Edition boundary: this draft contains material and an ending that differ from the theatrical release. Audit the screenplay text itself, not memory of the finished film.

Calibration scenes:

- PDF pages 7-16: ordinary couple behavior, race anxiety, smoking, a road accident, and a police encounter establish character protection, trust, and later exploitable vulnerabilities. Test whether exposition is embedded in present decisions and relationship behavior.
- PDF pages 67-78: a warning creates the decision to leave; relationship pressure and guilt are used to reverse that decision; the request for car keys reclassifies the situation. Test whether every strategy shift follows a new stimulus and changes the next action.
- PDF pages 87-98: escape uses previously demonstrated rules and actions, while the protagonist's old wound causes a costly choice. Test whether the climax solution was learned earlier and whether backstory produces behavior rather than explanation.

Do not imitate hypnosis, body transfer, the estate, the camera flash, or the visit-to-family plot. Transfer only the mechanism.

### The Social Network

- Authorship: screenplay by Aaron Sorkin; awards screenplay.
- Source identity: [Sony official film page](https://www.sonypictures.com/movies/thesocialnetwork) and [Academy award record](https://awardsdatabase.oscars.org/search/getresults?query=%7B%22FilmTitle%22%3A%22the+social+network%22%2C%22Sort%22%3A%222-Film+Title-Alpha%22%2C%22AwardShowNumberFrom%22%3A0%2C%22AwardShowNumberTo%22%3A0%2C%22Search%22%3A30%7D).
- Readable screenplay edition: [164-page screenplay PDF mirror](https://thescriptsavant.com/movies/The_Social_Network.pdf).

Calibration scenes:

- PDF pages 1-9: two people hear different meanings in the same conversation. Correction, interruption, literal interpretation, status anxiety, insult, apology, and refusal progressively destroy the relationship. Test whether dialogue is clear to the audience while remaining imperfect and character-specific.
- PDF pages 108-110: testimony, embarrassment, friendship, and legal tactics overlap. Test whether a line can answer the legal question, protect a relationship, and accidentally expose damaging information at the same time.
- PDF pages 145-152: earlier friendship, ownership, humiliation, money, and betrayal converge in confrontation. Test whether an emotional line also changes legal or relational action instead of serving as a speech.

Do not imitate Harvard, Facebook, depositions, rapid-fire Sorkin cadence, or any recognizable line. Transfer only the mechanics of misalignment, voice, tactical dialogue, and consequence.

## Draft Comparison Questions

Use the real scripts to ask, not to decorate the answer:

1. Can a reader retell the entire current story as action and consequence?
2. Does each important character choose the best action available to them, or does the plot require them to ignore an obvious option?
3. Does new information change strategy, leverage, or physical possibility?
4. Does dialogue reveal distinct ways of hearing, protecting, attacking, delaying, and misunderstanding?
5. Does the final choice cost something established earlier?
6. Does the ending feel both caused by prior events and chosen by a person?

A draft that merely resembles these films in tone, props, twists, dialogue speed, or subject matter fails calibration.

Quality labels are downstream of the cold-read gate. A draft with an unresolved `LOGIC FAIL`, `STORY WEAK/FAIL`, an opening-comprehension failure, or sustained writerly dialogue compression cannot be rated `strong`, `excellent`, or benchmark-passing because its props, structure, imagery, or scene craft are polished. Explicit user feedback that the opening is confusing, the dialogue does not sound human, or the overall logic does not hold sets user status to not accepted and returns the draft to the named upstream layer; exemplar comparison cannot overrule that result.
