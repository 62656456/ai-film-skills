# Future weapon and equipment systems route

Load this reference when the requested image includes weapons, armor, combat vehicles, defensive infrastructure, mission payloads, or a standardized military/rescue equipment family. Design a complete operating system, not a decorated gun.

## Safety and evidence boundary

- Keep this route at non-actionable film-design level. Define only the source-relevant mission, visible modules, user/body relation, energy, heat, load, logistics, service, condition, environment, camera, and story or reference function.
- Do not supply real-world construction steps, internal schematics, targeting algorithms, material recipes, fabrication tolerances, exploitable vulnerabilities, or performance numbers for range, penetration, energy, frequency, explosive yield, armor, or guidance.
- Mark current mechanisms as verified only where an authoritative source supports them. Treat future integration as constrained extrapolation and fictional effects as explicit hypotheses.
- Never copy a recognizable franchise weapon, armor, vehicle, ship, insignia, sound, color arrangement, or silhouette.

## Imagination and operational-grammar leap

Future equipment becomes genuinely new when a mechanism changes one or more consequential relations among sensing, governance, support, movement, energy, heat, replenishment, safety/civilian protection, architecture, ecology, or recovery—not when a current weapon receives new panels.

For amplitude II–III:

- write one world-exclusive visual thesis in which the equipment changes posture, route, timing, cooperation, or the built environment;
- combine one verified physical constraint, one non-adjacent workflow, and one explicit discontinuity in effect delivery, embodiment, network relation, power/thermal economy, or lifecycle;
- generate three functionally valid routes before selecting one, preserving all user/script locks and differing in at least one consequential open dimension such as embodiment/governance, support/reaction, echelon, effect topology, lifecycle, environment relation, or infrastructure consequence; do not force all dimensions to change;
- propagate the chosen system into at least three source-relevant visible consequences selected from body/roles, vehicle/base relation, logistics/lifecycle, safety/collateral, architecture, ecology, institution, timing, or recovery; do not invent every domain;
- show relationships across only the individual, group, vehicle, infrastructure, ecological, or distributed layers the brief spans and the frame can read; a single-echelon system need not grow a full hierarchy;
- run the contemporary-substitution test: if a present rifle, turret, armored vehicle, radar, or launcher can replace the system without changing blocking and behavior, redesign it.

This section never authorizes actionable weapon detail. Keep effects at cinematic system level while making source-relevant responsibility, energy, heat, reaction, collateral limits, operating tradeoffs, and body/environment relation clear. A persistent cost or active use event is required only when the screenplay contains one.

## System-of-systems card

For every dominant system covered by this reference, record only the applicable fields; mark an omitted field `not applicable` rather than inventing hardware, personnel, use, damage, or logistics:

1. **Echelon**: individual, squad/crew-served, vehicle/mecha, base/ship, or distributed network.
2. **Mission effect**: intercept, disable, cut, shield, breach, mark, suppress, tow, rescue, deny access, or protect a corridor. Keep the description high-level.
3. **Sensing and governance**: sensor or state source, responsible human/nonhuman/collective/autonomous layer, confidence/fault state, safety, and degraded mode. Automation changes responsibility; it does not make it irrelevant.
4. **Effect mechanism**: the source-supported or explicitly hypothetical high-level effect, without forcing kinetic mass, directed energy, drones, fields, or barriers.
5. **Power path**: source, buffer, distribution, isolation, and what other capability loses power during peak use.
6. **Heat and load path**: recoil/reaction, torque, structural support, cooling, heat capacity, radiator or exchanger, safe direction, and recovery time.
7. **Consumables and logistics**: only the ammunition/effect medium, energy storage, coolant/thermal mass, replacement components, transport, storage, and replenishment the mechanism actually uses.
8. **Body/agency and service path**: applicable grip, support, restraint, roles, access, embodiment, manipulator clearance, lifting/handling, escape, fault isolation, replacement, or repair boundary.
9. **Signature and collateral**: sound only in a medium, plume, flash, heat, dust, debris, electromagnetic interference, backblast, unsafe arc, friendly traffic, or habitat risk.
10. **Localized condition**: use, cleaning, corrosion, heat, handling, replacement, storage, and battle damage only where the current state supports them; concentrate traces at grip, mount, aperture, suspension, sensor, interface, cable bend, strike, or frequently replaced module.

Reject an effect or protective capability that has no visible support, energy/heat/load tradeoff, state envelope, role/body consequence, or relationship to the environment. Do not invent active use merely to display the tradeoff in an isolated reference image.

## Equipment spectrum

### Individual level

- Integrate only the source-required protection, sensing, communication, power, mobility assistance, medical access, tools, and effect modules as one carried load budget; do not add the whole suite by genre.
- When standardization, modularity, or replacement exists, use source-derived shared energy/data/material interfaces and mission modules; a sealed, bespoke, biological, passive, or disposable system need not use replaceable modules. Separate life-support from optional effects only where both exist.
- During active use, route reaction through the actual human/nonhuman body, support, vehicle, field, environment, or counter-motion relation; shoulders, hands, harnesses, monopods, braces, restraints, tethers, and counter-rotation are conditional examples.
- For repeated-use capability, show the applicable heat capacity, consumable, recharge, recovery, or other limiting state. A passive, single-use, stowed, or neutral asset may express its envelope without inventing firing or damage; no capability is unlimited without a paid mechanism.
- Derive control and feedback from body, environment, latency, authority, safety, and degraded operation. Local tactile, visor, gaze, voice, neural, remote, distributed, nonhuman, or autonomous control may be valid; physical state must independently reveal critical outcomes.
- In a narrative frame, prefer a source-required agent or system solving the actual beat over a hero weapon silhouette. In an explicit asset reference, a complete neutral view is valid when family identity, function, scale, applicable interfaces or relationships, support, and operating logic are clear.

### Squad, crew-served, and standardized family

- Build a family around shared operating/lifecycle logic rather than identical shells. Interfaces, energy packs, sensors, thermal modules, protection, maintenance tools, software, biological cues, infrastructure, procedures, or training are optional ways to express that grammar.
- When the system's scale requires distinct responsibilities, derive observation, governance/authorization, effect control, power/thermal, security, and casualty/safety functions; they may be combined, distributed, nonhuman, autonomous, or absent where inapplicable.
- When peak energy/heat exceeds what the embodied unit can carry, derive an external, distributed, environmental, infrastructure, sacrificial, time-buffered, or other explicit support relation; packs, sleds, tripods, vehicles, walls, and decks are examples.
- Show standardization through the applicable shared connection, scale envelope, material/state code, service/lifecycle method, module relation, behavior, or institution; connectors, swap envelopes, and carts are not mandatory. Avoid cloned users, identical condition, and unsupported parade layout.
- When the brief is narrative or operational, make only the source-required tactical relationship readable, such as overlapping observation, protected resupply, safe direction, relay, withdrawal, or casualty movement. A neutral asset records its family relation without inventing deployment. Do not provide real deployment instructions.

### Vehicle and mecha level

- Treat only the applicable mobility, sensing, occupied-body survival or autonomous protection, power, heat, mission effect, service strategy, and escape/recovery needs as competing volume and mass claims.
- Align reaction loads with a longitudinal frame, turret ring, keel, suspension, tracks, stabilizers, anchors, counter-rotating mass, or attitude-control system.
- Separate average propulsion power from short peak-effect buffering only when both exist. When the source captures active use or aftermath, show the applicable tradeoff: reduced mobility, isolated bus, opened exchanger, exposed radiator, depleted module, recovery interval, or another supported consequence. A stowed, passive, pre-use, or neutral reference must not invent a peak-effect event.
- Keep only the applicable sensors, occupied or autonomous control volume, payload, power buffer, cooling, consumables, and service strategy physically legible but not diagram-like. A crane is only one possible service solution.
- Derive the view from the selected scene function or asset-reference need. A hatch, wheel, stabilizer, service deck, cockpit, remote sensor, full diagnostic profile, or another anchor may be correct; reject merely dressing a centered present-day platform with new panels and neon.

### Base, ship, and distributed level

- Design a base-, ship-, or distributed-level system as infrastructure, including only the applicable sensing, authorization or autonomous governance, power, buffer, main structure, effect aperture/release point, safe envelope, heat rejection, service route, and replacement strategy.
- Derive responsibility from scale, law, species, automation, latency, and failure consequence. Tracking, authorization, power/thermal supervision, collateral clearance, and fault isolation may be separate, combined, nonhuman, or autonomous roles; do not force five human stations.
- In vacuum, an energy beam is not a colored light strip unless an intervening medium or explicit visible tracer explains it. Show the result, aperture state, heat state, or sensor response instead.
- Route reaction and torque into the foundation, truss, hull, flywheel, counter-mass, or attitude control. Keep radiators, arrays, thrusters, traffic, and crew outside unsafe arcs.
- In a narrative frame, avoid a complete symmetric superweapon display only when it erases the selected beat; derive the viewpoint from the actual agent/governance layer, affected body, protected space, institution, environment, or consequence. A source-motivated confrontation, ceremony, inspection, or symmetric relation may be complete; an asset reference uses diagnostic views without inventing maintenance.

## Energy, heat, and effect families

### Kinetic or electromagnetic

- For a kinetic system, show the applicable mass source/store and structural reaction. For an electromagnetic accelerator or field effect, show its actual conductors, field structure, buffer/isolation, heat path, and only the wear surfaces or replaceable modules its mechanism produces.
- In atmosphere, dust, pressure, flash, sound, and thermal disturbance require a source. In vacuum, use recoil, ejecta, local flash, vehicle attitude, and heat state rather than smoke or contrails.
- Avoid turning every future effect system into a long rifle or missile tube. Derive topology from its mechanism, energy/load path, operating envelope, body or infrastructure relation, protection, and service strategy; none of those requires one fixed outer silhouette.

### Directed energy

- Show applicable sensor quality, pointing stability, clear aperture, source-appropriate authorization or autonomous governance, energy source, heat capacity/cooling, contamination control, and recovery state.
- Keep the beam invisible in clean air or vacuum unless a specified medium scatters it. Make the effect readable through the applicable aperture/source state, target or protected-space response, heat state, governance/command or autonomous response, and line-of-sight protection; do not require every cue in one frame.
- Do not use colored laser ribbons, glowing coils, or unlimited continuous fire as proof of power.

### Interceptor, drone, and barrier systems

- Show only the applicable storage/deployment, communication or coordination, identification, safety envelope, recovery/disposal, replenishment, and faulted-unit handling; a persistent barrier or non-recoverable interceptor may use a different lifecycle.
- Let autonomy alter workload and responsibility while preserving only the source-relevant authorization/governance, uncertainty, abort/fail-safe, and friendly-traffic constraints.
- When standardization or servicing matters, make the relevant payload interfaces, lifecycle, and service strategy visible; avoid a cloud of identical drones as decorative background.

## Protection and support equipment

- Treat protection as a compromise among only the applicable threat, mobility, heat, visibility/sensing, pressure/chemistry, service/healing, medical/body access, and escape/release. Avoid an unsupported glossy full-body superhero shell; source-derived glossy protection remains valid.
- Route powered assistance through the actual body/contact/support, sensing/governance, inertia, clearance, and safe-state relation. Joints, harnesses, feet, doors, vehicles, and maintenance spaces apply only to bodies and environments that have them.
- Include countermeasure, medical, communications, environmental protection, tools, spare energy, and casualty handling when they affect the scene. A future soldier is not only a gun carrier.
- Use localized replacement, patching, wear, discoloration, contamination, healing, or impact evidence only when the requested current state and operating history produce it; a new, sterile, sealed, ceremonial, dormant, or one-shot asset may be unmarked.

## Camera and story/reference integration

- In a narrative frame, use the screenplay's selected function contract. Action may include authorization, exchange, shutdown, isolation, protection, withdrawal, rescue, or another named beat; discovery, relationship, establishing, aftermath, and suspense need not become firing or maintenance scenes.
- In an isolated asset/reference frame, show identity, function, scale, applicable interfaces/relationships, material/protection logic, shared family grammar when a family exists, and requested state without inventing a user or opponent.
- Derive camera or diagram/view relation from the source and deliverable: user body, affected space, public boundary, vehicle hatch, protected aperture, remote observation, neutral reference, or another justified anchor.
- Keep a body, interface, support, route, or consequence readable only when it belongs to the source. Background teams are required only in a populated busy operation.
- Use equipment to change posture, route, timing, access, protection, or choice when the story uses it. Do not make people idle decorations around a weapon.

## Route vetoes

Reject the image or prompt if any applicable item appears:

- a contemporary rifle, tank, missile truck, fighter, or naval gun with cosmetic panels and LEDs;
- centered heroic weapon product shot, full-body armored hero, parade lineup, or empty showroom that erases the selected narrative function; a source-motivated portrait, ceremony, inspection, or neutral asset reference may use complete/frontal geometry when diagnostically justified;
- unlimited capability, or missing source-relevant energy, thermal, reaction/load, consumable, service/lifecycle, and recovery constraints; do not require a consumable, maintenance event, or recovery interval when the mechanism and requested state do not use one;
- giant visible laser ribbon in vacuum, decorative muzzle flash, random sparks, or smoke without a medium;
- weapon, radiator, array, thruster, crew path, hatch, sensor, or safe arc occupying the same space;
- one embodied agent carrying vehicle-scale energy, cooling, support, and effect mass without an explicit assistance/distribution mechanism;
- cloned equipment with identical damage or a standardized family differentiated only by color;
- interface wallpaper, unsupported floating holograms, pseudo-text, universal red alarms, unmotivated universal teal-orange grade, or glossy black armor used as an unexplained genre default; any of these may appear only when the screenplay, material/light physics, and approved visual system specifically require it;
- copied franchise silhouette, signature weapon, helmet, ship, insignia, or color identity;
- amplitude II–III that remains a present rifle, turret, tank, fighter, launcher, or radar after labels, UI, setting, and color are removed;
- actionable real-world fabrication, performance, targeting, or vulnerability detail.
