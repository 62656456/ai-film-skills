# Functional production design

Use this reference whenever the scene contains people, organisms, tools, vehicles, habitats, interfaces, infrastructure, or machinery. Design first from the current script function, environment, body/system relation, operation, manufacture, and lived history. Work, maintenance, damage, and failure are optional consequences, not universal scene premises.

## Function chain

For each major system, define only applicable paths and mark the rest `not applicable`; passive tools, organisms, natural assets, sealed one-shot systems, and non-electronic equipment do not need invented sensors, displays, controls, communications, repair, or fallback:

1. **Mission or existence function**: what the system/object/place must accomplish or be now.
2. **Environment**: only the pressure, temperature, radiation, dust, atmosphere, gravity, chemistry, biology, and access limits that affect it.
3. **Load/support path**: where applicable mass, thrust, rotation, tension, compression, impact, buoyancy, growth, or distributed contact travels.
4. **Energy/power path**: generation/intake, storage, distribution, switching, or isolation only when energy is actively managed.
5. **Heat/material-exchange path**: only the relevant source, conduction, flow, radiation, insulation, phase change, metabolism, or sacrificial behavior.
6. **Body/use path**: approach, posture, reach, touch, restraint, accommodation, visibility, privacy, service access, and escape only where relevant.
7. **Information/control relation**: sensing, display/cue, control/governance, communication, or fallback only when the system uses them.
8. **State envelope**: normal, changing, degraded, failed, ceremonial, dormant, growing, or another script-relevant state, including recovery only when the source requires it.

Every consequential visible element must trace to at least one applicable function, constraint, body/world relation, origin/manufacture/growth process, operating history, or explicit hypothesis; it need not answer every path.

## Shape grammar

- Pressure vessels favor continuous shells, restrained openings, reinforced hatches, seals, and repeated service interfaces.
- Vacuum vehicles need no aerodynamic nose or wings unless they also enter an atmosphere or the shape serves another named function.
- Atmospheric vehicles derive lift, drag, buoyancy, rotor size, sealing, and stability from the local medium and gravity.
- Large orbital structures reveal the applicable load, rotation, deployment/assembly, transport, utility, and lifecycle strategy. Trusses, spokes, bearings, joints, hinges, cables, monolithic shells, fields, biological growth, or other explicit mechanisms are options rather than a fixed silhouette.
- Heavy surface systems reveal their applicable contact/support, slope or terrain relation, load transfer, environmental protection, access, and lifecycle strategy. Tracks, feet, anchors, counterweights, distributed buoyancy, buried foundations, or another supported solution are options rather than a required bundle.
- Calm large surfaces make scale legible. Do not cover every panel with equal-density vents, pipes, grooves, and glowing seams.

## Material grammar

Describe each material as **function → material → surface response → localized history**.

Examples:

- thermal protection → stitched multilayer blanket → irregular soft reflections → repaired seam and cutout around a service port;
- pressure boundary → coated aluminum or composite shell → broad quiet specular response → scratches and hand polish near a hatch;
- radiator → emissive panel → matte or controlled reflection → fluid connections and micrometeoroid patch;
- lunar boot interface → abrasion-resistant fabric and joint cover → dust-clogged folds → cleaning marks around the seal;
- Titan vessel hull → cryogenic-compatible insulated shell → dim wet hydrocarbon reflection → frost or residue only near a heat leak.

Reject generic rusty metal, uniform grime, procedural scratches, chrome-everything, plastic-black armor, and identical surface age.

## Operating, lived, and maintenance evidence

Credibility often appears in small differences caused by operation, bodies, culture, care, environment, and maintenance. Select only evidence that belongs to the current world and beat:

- removable covers and captive fasteners;
- numbered but non-legible service zones rather than decorative text;
- inspection witness marks, torque stripes, replacement parts, patch dates, seal protectors;
- tool clearances, footholds, glove-sized handles, restrained cables;
- clean zones beside dirty zones where crews repeatedly touch, brush, or replace components;
- heat discoloration only near thrusters, brakes, reactors, or high-current hardware;
- impact pits and patch plates on exposed surfaces;
- dust or liquid paths that respect gravity, electrostatics, flow, and shielding.

Do not add readable brand marks, serial numbers, logos, or random warning text unless explicitly requested.

## Interface and UI gate

Apply [future-interface-systems.md](future-interface-systems.md) whenever an interface is visible or story-relevant.

- In a narrative/world frame, first pass the selected function-specific no-text evidence gate; a display cannot be the only evidence of the action, discovery, relationship, spatial function, aftermath, identity, or system state. For an explicitly isolated UI/HMI asset, apply its declared system/task model and internal-redundancy gate instead; a screen-only or nonvisual asset is allowed.
- Put information on a task- and environment-derived carrier: integrated matte display, visor optics, protected window, relief topology, mechanical mimic board, sight channel, moving scale, or projected surface with a visible emitter.
- Connect every visible interface through its source-derived chain: `system state/sensor → presentation or cue → responsible human, nonhuman, collective, or autonomous interpretation → input/authorization when applicable → effector or flow → physical/world feedback`. A human gaze/hand chain is one conditional case, not the default.
- In a narrative/world frame, match the interface topology, orientation, node order, flow direction, and affected region to the visible machine, body, facility, city, ecology, or event. In an isolated interface asset, match them to the declared system/task model. A generic interface that could move unchanged to another system fails.
- Use restrained functional state color with shape, line, position, texture, rhythm, or mechanical-state redundancy. The screen is not the scene's decorative light source unless story logic requires it.
- Preserve only the carrier's applicable occlusion/access, reflection or signal interference, intensity/readability limits, orientation, depth, body/sense access, lifecycle, and degraded fallback. Gloves, touch reach, screens, and maintenance are conditional. Physical does not mean accidental retro.
- Keep precise text for post. In generated images, use large topology, state blocks, paths, needles, lights, and physical positions; small pseudo-text, bare percentages, and random seven-segment values cannot carry the plot.

## People and organisms in use

- Give each visible person or organism a source-derived objective, attention, relation, route or stillness, contact/gesture, and environmental constraint. A tool or destination is required only when the beat contains one.
- When pressure, acceleration, inertia, gravity, temperature, wind, protection, ceremony, fatigue, calm, or another state matters, make posture reveal that applicable condition; do not add strain or protective behavior to an ordinary scene without cause.
- When the source establishes a busy inhabited scene, create two or more continuous background systems with different routes and purposes. Quiet, intimate, empty, ceremonial, or reference scenes require no crowd.
- Keep safety behavior visible: tethers, foot restraints, buddy checks, suit ports, handrails, barriers only where they follow real operations, and controlled access.
- Reject idle or cloned staging that erases the selected function. A source-motivated portrait, inspection, ceremony, confrontation, lineup, deliberate stillness, or face-forward relation may be valid when it is the beat rather than a generic hero template.

## Scale and composition

- Prove size through source-relevant occupied, inhabited, traversed, biological, or serviced surfaces; bodies, hatches, furniture, vehicles, repeated units, tool reach, terrain, displacement, and interfaces are options rather than a fixed list.
- Crop a large structure when its continuation beyond frame increases credibility; do not force every megastructure into a complete centered silhouette.
- Use one dominant perceptual relationship appropriate to the selected function: action, evidence, relational spacing, inhabited geography, aftermath trace, or asset identity.
- Let secondary design support that relationship. Remove competing objects that do not clarify story, world, operation, or intended ambiguity.

## Anti-template rules

Do not default to:

- rainy night and wet reflective floor;
- teal-orange grade;
- central giant robot or ship facing camera;
- endless hangar corridor;
- dense white work lights and red alarms;
- glossy black panels with blue LED seams;
- hologram walls;
- smoke and volumetric beams in every interior;
- military personnel standing still around a hero machine.

For variants, preserve all source facts and approved locks. Change only declared open variables, and make routes differ in at least one consequential mechanism, topology, body/system relation, environment response, production/view relation, or light/color logic rather than only renaming the vehicle or recoloring it.

## Production-design audit

- Can every dominant shape be traced to mission, environment, load, heat, pressure, access, or assembly?
- Are details concentrated at source-relevant contact, sensing, force, energy, biology, access, or information zones rather than uniformly distributed?
- Where power and waste heat matter, do they have visible or explicitly bounded routes?
- In a narrative/world frame, does the selected function remain readable with UI text hidden, and are interface modality/governance, real topology, feedback, and world evidence tied to the same task? For an isolated UI/HMI asset, does its declared system/task model and internal redundancy pass instead?
- When use history or wear is present, does it match the environment and source state? A new, sterile, ceremonial, dormant, or abstract asset need not be worn.
- When the scene is populated and busy, do background people and machines keep working? When it is quiet or empty, is that condition protected?
- Is there one dominant function-specific visual relationship and a controlled hierarchy?
