# Research-first inspiration engine

Use this reference after the screenplay/source has been analyzed and its open design gaps are known. Research constrains mechanisms and expands options; it does not choose the story, future tier, world route, silhouette, palette, or style by popularity.

## Research-first gate

Preserve explicit source facts and approved locks, then formulate the smallest research questions needed to solve open gaps. Before making a new design inference about mechanism, future degree, world, environment, system route, form, palette, interface, or production method:

1. run an actual search;
2. open the source itself;
3. record what it supports and does not support;
4. separate verified fact, constrained extrapolation, explicit hypothesis, and film-production lesson;
5. extract transferable causal mechanisms and discard literal appearance.

A search-result snippet, model memory, second-hand summary, or unopened citation is not evidence. If usable sources cannot be opened, stop before a final design-changing prompt or image call unless the user explicitly authorizes a labeled offline preliminary.

An approved, versioned evidence card may be reused for later shots in the same locked world while its assumptions, scope, and source currency remain valid. Research again when the script introduces a new mechanism, environment, body, interface, system route, or contradiction.

## Research question card

Start with:

- `source/beat`: current authority source and scene function;
- `open gap`: the unresolved decision, not a desired look;
- `decision affected`: world, environment, mechanism, body, infrastructure, interface, manufacture, human factors, production, color source, or another named choice;
- `what would falsify`: evidence that would make a route invalid;
- `minimum useful answer`: the constraint or mechanism needed before design can proceed.

Good questions ask how something operates, fails, is built, maintained, sensed, controlled, experienced, or filmed. Weak questions ask what “future X looks like.”

## Source classes and maturity

Prefer the strongest available evidence for each claim:

- primary scientific paper, experiment, mission data, standard, regulator, official technical report, or manufacturer documentation for engineering and human-factors claims;
- official program, laboratory, university, agency, or company material for maturity and operating context;
- independent technical or industry evidence for a non-adjacent workflow or production mechanism;
- official production-design, cinematography, VFX, or interface case study for how an effect was staged or captured;
- weak/secondary material only when stronger sources are unavailable, with the gap stated.

Record maturity as operational, demonstrated/tested, prototype, funded study, early research, speculative proposal, or fictional production lesson. Never upgrade a paper concept to deployed technology.

## Search-evidence card

For each opened source record:

- exact query and search date;
- title, author or organization, publication/update date, direct link or stable identifier, and opened section;
- source class and maturity;
- exact supported claim;
- transferable mechanism or workflow;
- deliverable-visible physical, material, behavioral, spatial, or signal trace;
- limit, uncertainty, conflict, failure, cost, or missing evidence;
- screenplay fact or open gap it informs;
- literal silhouette, layout, palette, marking, anatomy, or signature composition that must not transfer.

If research contradicts the screenplay, return the conflict to the authority layer. Do not quietly redesign the story around the research.

## Finding pool before selection

Do not search only for confirmation of the first idea. For a project-identity decision, high-imagination request, or materially underspecified mechanism, retain genuinely different routes long enough to compare:

- story and emotional fit;
- physical compatibility and explicit hypotheses;
- maturity and evidence gaps;
- transformative leverage;
- effect on bodies, work, infrastructure, institution, ecology, and time;
- substitution resistance;
- one-frame readability and information order;
- production translation and the applicable photographed/practical, miniature, animated, illustrated, sensor, UI, or asset grounding relation;
- originality risk and source-form contamination.

For a routine shot inside an approved visual bible, a narrow search may be sufficient. Do not force three sources, a non-adjacent mechanism, or a new speculative leap into every scene.

## Two synthesis levels

### Bounded synthesis

Use for literal adaptations, routine continuity shots, diagnostics, and small open gaps. Select the strongest relevant mechanism, respect its limits, and translate only the deliverable-visible consequence the task needs.

### Amplified synthesis

Use for new world identity, flagship concept design, or explicit high-imagination scope. Build at least three transformation pitches that differ in an open topology, scale relation, agent/body relation, infrastructure, institution, ecology, manufacture, lifecycle, or time dimension while preserving locks.

The selected pitch should combine:

- one verified anchor or explicitly labeled hypothesis and its non-negotiable cost;
- one genuinely non-adjacent transformation mechanism when it creates new behavior rather than visual collage;
- one script-derived dramatic pressure, desire, discovery, relationship, or social consequence;
- at least three deliverable-visible downstream consequences for amplitude II–III;
- a grounding anchor and layer/extension plan appropriate to the selected production route: practical/photographed contact for live action when present, miniature logic, animation/illustration construction, sensor projection, UI state model, asset-view system, or another declared method.

The screenplay may require wonder, intimacy, secrecy, ceremony, boredom, conflict, labor, play, or terror. Do not replace “dramatic pressure” with a mandatory human dilemma, failure, repair, rescue, or sacrifice.

## Source-form deletion and topology divergence

Research supplies invariants and operations, not final appearance. Before locking a form:

1. list the source traits that must be removed;
2. derive geometry from the selected environment, loads, access, manufacture, body relation, and history;
3. for amplified synthesis, compare at least three functionally valid topologies;
4. reject the first familiar industrial or franchise silhouette;
5. test the design after removing source names, UI, palette, grade, and location labels.

If a present-day factory, crane, robot arm, vehicle, weapon, pressure door, or familiar animal can replace the central system without breaking the story and blocking, the flagship concept has not transformed enough. This substitution test is not required to make every ordinary continuity shot visually unprecedented.

## Inspiration mechanism card

When full synthesis is triggered, output:

- current source/beat and open design question;
- finding pool with provenance, maturity, limits, and no-copy traits;
- rejected routes and why;
- selected verified anchor or hypothesis;
- selected non-adjacent transformation, if used;
- script-derived dramatic pressure or audience experience;
- environment and body deformation;
- operating/manufacturing history;
- topology candidates and selected route;
- visible downstream consequences;
- route-appropriate grounding, layer, and extension strategy;
- contemporary-substitution result;
- evidence gaps and approval status.

Keep source names and citations in this card. The image prompt contains only transformed observable mechanisms.

## Workflow

1. Read the script authority and visual-translation cards.
2. Preserve facts, locks, prohibited rewrites, dramatic function, and information order.
3. Convert open gaps into research questions and falsifiers.
4. Search and open sources; build evidence cards.
5. Classify claims, maturity, limits, conflicts, and no-copy traits.
6. Decide bounded or amplified synthesis from scope, not habit.
7. Build and compare the required finding routes.
8. Select mechanisms from story, physics, production, and originality—not result frequency.
9. Derive physical, environmental, institutional, bodily, informational, and deliverable-visible consequences.
10. Delete source appearance; for amplified synthesis, run topology divergence and substitution tests.
11. Mark the result PROVISIONAL or CANDIDATE until user approval.
12. Keep citations outside the model-facing prompt.

## Gates

Reject or revise if any applicable answer is no:

1. Did source analysis and gap identification happen before search?
2. Did actual search and source opening happen before new design inference?
3. Can each research claim be traced to an opened source and relevant section?
4. Are maturity, limitations, conflicts, and evidence gaps stated?
5. Did research answer an open gap rather than override the screenplay?
6. Were source mechanism and source appearance separated?
7. For identity/high-imagination work, were genuinely different routes compared rather than one prototype dramatized?
8. Does the selected synthesis fit the dramatic function and intended information order?
9. For amplitude II–III, do at least three visible consequences follow from one paid rule without a hidden second miracle?
10. Does the final design avoid recognizable source/franchise form and pass the applicable substitution test?
11. Are unapproved results still labeled provisional or candidate?
