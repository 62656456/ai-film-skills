# Original humanoid combat-mecha aesthetic

Load this reference only when combat, armed defense, interception, deterrence, battlefield survival, or military responsibility defines a mecha's identity. Load it after `mecha-and-megafauna.md`; also load `future-weapon-systems.md` when a weapon/effect system is visible or design-changing. A mecha merely appearing in a war zone, carrying a self-defense tool, or performing civilian rescue does not automatically trigger this route.

This module owns combat identity, humanoid role readability, armor/inner-frame hierarchy, second silhouette, multi-mecha divergence, cinematic combat-mecha presentation, and reference abstraction. `mecha-and-megafauna.md` still owns support, mass, actuation, motion, operation, scale, and environment response. `future-weapon-systems.md` still owns effect, authorization, energy/heat/reaction, safety, lifecycle, logistics, and the non-actionable boundary.

## Example firewall

This is a design capability, not a prompt library, character roster, fixed scene menu, or five-image reproducer.

Prior images, prompts, concept groups, famous mecha, model-kit lines, collectible designs, live-action giant-robot films, and user examples are **illustrative evidence** unless the user explicitly locks a trait. First record what problem each example solves, then extract a transferable relationship. Never transfer an example's count, name, faction, scene, terrain, palette, weapon shape, limb count, posture, composition, camera, logo, or signature combination by default.

Use this chain:

`reference attraction → upper-level visual property → current mission reason → newly derived structural implementation → literal traits to delete`

Reference roles may include:

- combat-character readability and memorable humanoid proportion;
- understandable inner-frame, joint, armor, and assembly hierarchy;
- strong but ordered second silhouette and equipment identity;
- giant mass, human touch, operating burden, and cinematic environment response.
- wearable armor, powered exoskeleton, human-operated loader, cockpit walker, mobile humanoid machine, giant mecha, spacecraft, ship, or mobile-infrastructure relations across the full human-to-machine scale continuum.

These are roles, not brands or prompt tokens. Named references stay in the analysis card and never enter the model-facing prompt.

The reference pool is open-ended: any mecha, powered armor, exoskeleton, loader, robot, vehicle, aircraft, spacecraft, ship, mobile structure, model, film prop, or real mechanism may be researched when it answers a current design gap. No source is automatically authoritative for every layer. Open and inspect the actual source, assign only the role it can support, state its limits, and delete its recognizable form before synthesis.

## Cross-project author grammar

Combat mecha share a design grammar, not a common body or skin:

1. **Responsibility enters the body.** Interception, suppression, breakthrough, escort, protection, denial, reconnaissance, or another source-defined responsibility must alter body plan, space occupation, movement, sensing, or protection—not only the handheld weapon.
2. **Combat geometry is readable.** Threat direction, protected/affected space, sensing, force transfer, reaction, turning, effect release, and escape/safe direction shape orientation, negative space, mass distribution, and current pose.
3. **Humanoid dominance remains structural.** When the source requests a humanoid combat mecha, establish a readable `head/neck → chest → torque waist → pelvis/hips → two arms → two legs` load-and-motion chain even when damage, terrain, crop, or pose hides parts. Do not drift into an ape, quadruped, construction rig, or tank-on-legs unless the source explicitly selects that topology.
4. **Hero readability and mass coexist.** Character hierarchy may be bold, elegant, compact, or severe, but support, inertia, reaction, heat, recovery time, and the operator's limits still pay for it.
5. **Calm masses and high-information nodes form rhythm.** Large load/protection surfaces remain legible; actuation, force, sensing, heat, access, crew, maintenance, and payload exchange nodes concentrate detail. Uniform greeble fails.
6. **Protection is risk zoning.** Armor, isolation, redundancy, sacrificial layers, replaceable modules, and exposed working organs appear where threat, mission, maintenance, and motion require them—not as uniform armor clothing.
7. **State is visible in body and material.** Deployment, maneuver, observation, bracing, effect delivery, thermal recovery, degraded withdrawal, or aftermath must alter support, joint locks, heat exchange, sensing, load state, damage, wear, or environment response. Red lights, captions, smoke, and muzzle effects cannot carry state alone.
8. **Human/automation responsibility is embodied.** Single operator, crew, remote control, distributed operation, supervised autonomy, or no occupant are all valid, but command latency, filtering, degraded mode, access, escape, and authority must agree with the machine.
9. **Environment redesigns the machine.** Sea, ice, vacuum, low gravity, soft ground, dense city, forest, or alien medium changes support, sealing, sensing, heat, protection, movement, and safety rather than only the background.
10. **Color has duties, not fixed hues.** It may encode material family, state, authority, hazard, heat, faction institution, or environment adaptation. There is no default combat-mecha palette.
11. **The camera stands inside the relationship.** Narrative frames derive viewpoint from protected space, danger boundary, operator, opponent sightline, infrastructure, or consequence. Only an explicit asset/reference request defaults to neutral full-body display.

The project remains free to choose body topology, limb count, scale, support medium, actuation, role, weapon/effect family, crew, protection, manufacture, materials, palette, faction marks, current state, environment, camera, and production route.

## Human–mecha scale relation

In combat imagery, human–mecha scale is a primary story, design, and camera variable rather than a decorative ruler. There is no universal correct height or ratio. A wearable armored body, powered exoskeleton, human-operated loader, cockpit walker, mobile humanoid machine, city-scale giant, spacecraft, or ship-scale combat body creates a different relationship among vulnerability, agency, touch, command, maintenance, danger radius, and viewer distance.

Record:

```text
human_scale_and_visibility
machine_scale_class
occupied_remote_or_unoccupied_relation
contact_access_and_escape
intermediate_scale_anchors
protected_threatened_or_affected_space
tactical_and_emotional_consequence
camera_distance_height_and_crop
what_the_viewer_can_read_at_that_scale
```

- Decide whether a person must be readable as a face/body/action, a small but purposeful operator, an occupied cockpit/access system, a vehicle/door/platform chain, or intentionally absent.
- Use at least three appropriate connected scale proofs when giant scale matters. People alone are insufficient, and adding people is not mandatory when another relation is more truthful.
- In battle scenes, scale must alter tactics, cover, danger radius, weapon/effect support, movement, evacuation, command latency, human burden, and the camera's ability to witness the event.
- A close person–machine relationship may require a smaller machine or intermediate access structures; disaster-scale machines may require vehicles, decks, buildings, gates, ships, or travel time so people do not collapse into meaningless pixels.
- Re-evaluate scale whenever story, environment, combat condition, operator relation, or intended emotion changes. Re-evaluation may keep the same scale when continuity or function justifies it; change is not mandatory.

## Request relationship mode

Before designing, classify the request:

- `SINGLE_FLAGSHIP`: one new combat mecha or one selected route;
- `SAME_WORLD_FAMILY`: different models sharing an approved civilization/manufacturing grammar;
- `SAME_MECHA_VARIANTS`: one locked machine with named variables changed;
- `INDEPENDENT_STORY_SET`: separate machines, worlds/stories, emotions, environments, or visual directions.

Do not infer a relationship mode from image count alone. A different story or scene does **not** automatically require a different machine, style, function, or body: first identify continuity, project/faction ownership, time, mission change, environment, damage/state, and the user's explicit intent. The same machine may cross different stories; a family may share strong DNA; unrelated worlds may independently converge on similar solutions. Conversely, a new responsibility or world may require a completely new body. Record what remains, what changes, and the causal reason. Use `INDEPENDENT_STORY_SET` only when the user asks for independent/different machines or when separate project authorities genuinely have no shared identity. When one solution is requested, do not force three or five outputs. When a family is approved, reuse its grammar rather than rediscovering flagship identities.

## Visual-desire and candidate gate

For a new flagship or open combat-mecha identity, propose at least three genuinely different visual theses before locking one. The number is a divergence aid, not a fixed output count. Skip forced divergence when the user chose a route or an approved family already owns identity.

When the user requests bolder design, or a previous result remains a safe generic armored humanoid, use the global imagination amplitude deliberately. At amplitude II–III, at least one candidate must change a foundational relationship—human/machine scale, body/support topology, mission geometry, operator embodiment, second silhouette, environment coupling, protection system, movement, or recovery—across at least three visible axes while paying a coherent physical/world cost. Boldness is not more spikes, armor fragments, glowing lines, weapons, or brand references.

Each thesis must state:

`first-read relationship → why the body must organize this way → dominant calm mass → highest-information node → current combat state → visible limitation/cost → what an ordinary armored humanoid cannot substitute`

Candidates must differ in consequential open axes such as body/support topology, combat responsibility, threat/effect geometry, movement/stability, crew/automation, survival/protection, energy/heat/recovery, logistics/lifecycle, environment coupling, or family relationship. Preserve rejected routes and why.

### Humanoid body and role

- Derive proportion from engagement distance, terrain, weapon/effect support, protection, acceleration, stability, human accommodation, and story role. Do not impose one head count, waist width, or hero ratio across projects.
- Keep head, neck, chest, torque waist, pelvis, hips, arms, and legs structurally readable. A crop may hide parts, but the remaining load chain must let the viewer infer a humanoid body.
- The head/sensor carrier, chest/torso, shoulder field, waist/pelvis, forearm, leg, and foot must each serve a distinct structural or combat relationship. A memorable head alone cannot rescue a generic body.
- Temporarily hide removable weapons. If combat responsibility, survivability, sensing/governance, force path, and movement envelope disappear, redesign the body: it is only an ordinary mecha carrying a weapon.

### Structural and armor hierarchy

Build from inside out:

1. primary load frame and bearing path;
2. actuation, braking, locking, energy, heat, sensing, and control modules;
3. local reinforcement and moving subframes;
4. protection, sacrificial, sealed, and replaceable armor;
5. identity, state, wear, repair, and environment history.

Movement must propagate through bearing, actuator/linkage, frame, sliding/splitting armor, hose/cable allowance, and final lock state without interpenetration. Use large calm armor fields for primary readability and concentrate density at real working nodes.

### Hydraulic and linkage credibility

Visible hydraulic structure must be complete enough to explain force, not decorate gaps:

- distinguish cylinder barrel, piston rod, tie rod, and mechanical linkage;
- connect both cylinder ends to load-bearing members using an applicable clevis, trunnion, eye/spherical joint, supported pin, bushing, and retention solution;
- align the cylinder's push/pull plane with the joint or use an explicit multi-axis accommodation;
- let a main bearing carry joint shear while cylinders/linkages create torque;
- route rigid lines along structure and use short protected flexible hose only across moving joints, with bend allowance and no pinch, abrasion, recoil, or hot-zone conflict;
- include local manifold/isolation/accumulator and service access only when that actuation family needs them;
- use mechanical locks/stops so a static or failed pose does not depend on unexplained trapped pressure;
- make damage originate at a credible mount, pin, seal, hose clamp/connector, rod surface, bearing, frame, armor, or heat path and propagate with gravity and load.

Hydraulic details remain visual-development mechanisms, not a manufacturable specification.

### Combat identity, second silhouette, and equipment

- Let sensing, protection, recoil/reaction support, heat, mobility, payload access, counterbalance, logistics, or mission equipment produce the second silhouette.
- Equipment uses a coherent family of hardpoints, locks, energy/data/working-medium interfaces, load paths, and stowage. Do not attach ornaments or weapons without body consequence.
- A removed or stowed weapon may leave a readable lock, reaction support, wear, cap, or interface; the body must retain identity without the prop.
- Cultural or faction identity may enter armor architecture, second silhouette, manufacture, replacement logic, and material coding. It cannot overwrite physics or copy a familiar crest, face, chest, backpack, weapon, or color-blocking system.
- Weapons/effects remain non-actionable: no performance numbers, targeting algorithms, vulnerabilities, construction drawings, or manufacturing steps.

## Cinematic scene and light contract

A cinematic combat-mecha image is a story moment, not a model showcase.

1. Select action/decision, discovery, relationship, establishing, aftermath, or asset/reference as the primary visual contract.
2. State who or what wants what, the obstacle, the irreversible/current threshold, and the affected person/space/system.
3. Put at least three appropriate scale relations into one connected geography; people are not mandatory, but when present they need purpose rather than acting as colored rulers.
4. Derive camera height, distance, lens tendency, crop, occlusion, and completeness from information order and emotional relation. Do not default to centered three-quarter full-body hero views.
5. When the deliverable is meant to validate a new combat-mecha design, declare the minimum identity anchors that must remain visible at normal viewing size. Crop and occlusion are welcome for cinema, but they cannot hide every carrier of author identity. Unless the source intentionally withholds identity, retain a readable sensor/head relation, one chest/waist/pelvis rhythm or equivalent body-plan relation, and one second-silhouette/limb/load relation. If the selected shot cannot carry both story and identity, separate the cinematic story frame from the neutral/diagnostic design view instead of compromising both.
6. Write one light-narrative sentence: `source and time → physical path/medium → what it reveals or withholds → emotional meaning`.
7. Do not default to sunset, backlight, crepuscular rays, rain night, teal-orange, searchlights, sparks, or explosion illumination. A dead/abandoned machine, active defense, quiet patrol, maintenance, and last stand require different light semantics.
8. Environment must react to support, mass, heat, displacement, corrosion, contamination, damage, or recovery. It is not wallpaper.

## Independent multi-mecha divergence

For `INDEPENDENT_STORY_SET`, build a divergence matrix before prompts or images. Each row records:

`story responsibility | world/environment | emotional function | light logic | body/support topology | head/sensor relation | chest/waist/pelvis rhythm | shoulder/second silhouette | movement | equipment/effect | crew/automation | palette/material | state/damage | camera relation`

Independent rows must have different story responsibility and materially different body/second-silhouette logic. Do not reuse one head/chest/shoulder/waist/leg body with new armor, weapon, palette, weather, or background, and do not reuse one centered composition or one light setup with recoloring. This strict divergence applies only after `INDEPENDENT_STORY_SET` is justified. For the same machine or family, continuity and shared grammar are expected; require only the changes caused by mission, environment, era, damage, load, or approved variant scope. Similarity is not a failure when its cause is explicit and source-authorized.

Run three tests:

- `weapon-hidden`: remove removable equipment; does each identity remain distinct?
- `skin-hidden`: remove color, marks, text, and environment; do body plan and author grammar remain readable?
- `sample-leak`: did an earlier example group reappear without source justification in count, order, scene, body, palette, pose, or composition?

If several candidates are the same shell with different packs, single-image scores cannot make the set pass.

## Vetoes

Reject or redesign when any applicable condition appears:

- ordinary armored humanoid plus removable weapon, with no combat identity in the body;
- humanoid request becomes an ape, quadruped, construction rig, or tank-on-legs without source authorization;
- a request that requires independent identities returns multiple mecha differing only by weapon, backpack, color, damage, weather, or background; or a same-machine/family request changes core identity without a source-authorized cause;
- battle identity depends on a hero pose, glowing chest, gun muzzle, red alarm, smoke, sparks, or explanatory text;
- uniform armor, uniform chrome, or uniform greeble erases structural hierarchy;
- actuation has floating rods, missing endpoints, impossible hose routing, no joint axis, no armor clearance, or no load path;
- mass, weapon/effect reaction, heat, safety, operator burden, or environment response is absent;
- a narrative frame is a centered full-body model/collectible display;
- a design-validation frame uses cinematic crop/occlusion to hide every declared identity anchor, making head/sensor, body-plan rhythm, and second silhouette impossible to judge;
- light uses a universal epic formula instead of the source's time, weather, material, and emotional function;
- recognizable franchise head, face, chest, shoulder, backpack, weapon, palette, mark, pose, or signature composition survives source-form deletion;
- an earlier example set leaks into a new request as a fixed count, menu, sequence, scene, palette, body, or composition;
- real-world actionable weapon design or manufacturing detail is exposed;
- internal score, prompt compliance, or image existence is called user approval.
