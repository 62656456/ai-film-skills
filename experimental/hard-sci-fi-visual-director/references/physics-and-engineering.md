# Physics and engineering gate

Use this reference for every design-changing task involving a physical world or system, but answer only the dimensions that can affect the current screenplay beat. Build from causal state rather than a collection of science-fiction objects; do not turn a quiet, relational, atmospheric, or reference scene into an engineering demonstration.

## Evidence labels

- **Verified fact**: supported by an authoritative mission, engineering, or scientific source.
- **Constrained extrapolation**: a future system derived from verified mechanisms and stated assumptions.
- **Explicit hypothesis**: a story-world invention whose consequences are defined consistently.

Never promote an extrapolation or hypothesis to verified fact. If exact values are unknown, use qualitative bounds and state the uncertainty.

## Universal physics card

Before visual design, answer:

1. **Medium**: vacuum, tenuous atmosphere, dense atmosphere, liquid, pressurized interior, or mixed boundary.
2. **Gravity**: source, direction, and approximate level; natural, rotational, acceleration-derived, or microgravity.
3. **Reference frame**: what is moving relative to what; which body or structure defines the camera frame.
4. **Pressure and temperature**: what must be sealed, insulated, heated, cooled, or allowed to vent.
5. **Light**: the source hierarchy required by star, sky scattering, planetshine, surface bounce, practical emitters, biology, or another explicit world mechanism.
6. **Power and heat**: where usable energy originates and where waste heat goes.
7. **Motion**: thrust, reaction force, inertia, friction, buoyancy, drag, or structural loading.
8. **Operating state**: normal, changing, dormant, ceremonial, growing, degraded, failed, or recovering; record visible consequence and safety only when the source uses them.

Every introduced physical claim must create a consistent visible or deliberately off-frame consequence. Require at least two readable cause-effect pairs for a technical action, engineering-system, or high-amplitude physical event. A quiet discovery, relationship, atmosphere, aftermath, or isolated asset may need only the causal relations it actually introduces; do not add machinery or failure merely to reach a count. Examples:

- short RCS pulse changes attitude while loose items preserve inertia;
- a loaded truss deflects while restraints and bearings carry the force;
- a hot system opens louvers or uses a radiator while adjacent insulation remains closed;
- low gravity lengthens a step but does not reduce the carried tool's inertia;
- a pressure boundary creates seals, latches, inspection marks, and controlled venting;
- dense atmosphere softens distant contrast and increases aerodynamic drag.

## Medium gate

### Vacuum

- No atmospheric haze, wind, rain, drifting smoke, billowing dust cloud, aerodynamic contrail, or sound-visible wave.
- Sunlight is a distant hard source. Shadows remain sharp unless a large reflected source or nearby luminous body changes them.
- Distance alone does not desaturate or soften through air. Use exposure, focus, occlusion, scale, surface response, and sensor limits.
- Ejecta and loose particles follow ballistic or inertial paths rather than curling in air.
- External waste heat leaves mainly by radiation; smoke stacks and convective cooling are invalid.

### Atmosphere

- Derive haze, contrast, sky color, shadow softness, wind, precipitation, and drag from composition, density, temperature, and illumination.
- Never import Earth-blue sky, water clouds, white foam, or familiar weather without route evidence.
- Combustion needs fuel and oxidizer. An oxygen-poor world cannot support an open terrestrial flame without a supplied oxidizer.

### Pressurized interior

- Air can carry smoke, condensation, sound, cloth motion, and convection, but only if a source exists.
- Doors, hatches, suits, and ducts reveal pressure zoning. A decompression event changes loose objects, vapor, sound behavior, and human posture.
- Artificial gravity must have a source. Do not give a microgravity cabin a universal floor by habit.

## Force, motion, and human behavior

- Weight changes with gravity; mass and inertia do not. Heavy tools remain difficult to start and stop in low gravity.
- In microgravity, every strong action needs a reaction path: handrail, foot restraint, tether, mechanical arm, body counter-rotation, or thruster.
- Free objects preserve velocity and angular momentum. They do not fall toward a screen edge.
- Docking and proximity operations are slow relative-motion events even though both vehicles move rapidly around the planet.
- Rotation-derived gravity points away from the axis. The outer rim is down; the hub approaches microgravity.
- Thruster plumes can heat, load, contaminate, or endanger nearby structures. Do not point them casually at radiators, arrays, crew, or docking surfaces.

## Power and waste heat

- Match power to the environment: solar only with adequate flux and orientation; storage for darkness; radioisotope or nuclear sources where long darkness or weak sunlight makes sense.
- Show heat management: radiator view to deep space, thermal straps, heat pipes, louvers, insulation, protected batteries, or fluid loops.
- Distinguish solar arrays from radiators. Arrays face the light source; radiators seek a cold view and avoid plume or solar heating.
- High-power industry requires conspicuous heat rejection. No amount of fictional efficiency eliminates waste heat without an explicit story-world mechanism.

## Functional material gate

- Multi-layer insulation appears as stitched, wrinkled, attached blanket material with cutouts and fasteners, not as decorative gold foil.
- Radiators appear as functional panels with clear orientation and plumbing, not glowing wings.
- Pressure vessels favor continuous load-bearing forms, reinforced openings, seals, and limited large windows.
- Details concentrate at joints, hatches, thermal zones, load paths, work areas, and repair points. Reject uniform greeble.
- Wear follows environment: lunar abrasion and adhesive dust; LEO atomic oxygen and impact pits; heat discoloration near thrusters; hand and tool polish near service zones; no rain rust in vacuum.

## Scale gate

When scale is story- or asset-relevant, use at least two independent proofs appropriate to the subject:

- child or adult body, hand, glove, handrail, hatch, standard interface, vehicle component, furniture, domestic object, organism, or carried pack;
- repeated structural bays, solar-cell units, radiator panels, tool sizes, service vehicles;
- occupied walkway or work envelope;
- known gravity or motion consequence over a visible distance.

Do not use a wide lens, tiny silhouettes, or the adjective monumental as the only proof.

## Six-question audit

Reject the prompt if any applicable answer is missing:

1. Is there an atmosphere, and therefore which haze, dust, smoke, rain, plume, or sky effects are physically allowed?
2. Where does gravity come from and which way is down for people, liquid, tools, and debris?
3. Where does power come from and where does waste heat go?
4. Do thrusters, arrays, radiators, antennas, docking ports, windows, and crew paths avoid functional conflict?
5. Are scale proofs and causal relationships sufficient for the selected beat and every introduced technical claim, without inventing extra systems merely to satisfy a count?
6. Does wear arise from this environment rather than a universal dirty-future filter?
