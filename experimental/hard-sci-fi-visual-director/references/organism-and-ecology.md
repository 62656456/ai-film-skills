# Nonhuman organism and ecology derivation

Use this reference whenever a task designs, depicts, diagnoses, or changes a nonhuman organism, alien life, speculative ecology, biosignature, symbiosis, colony, biological architecture, or organism-mediated environment. Use [mecha-and-megafauna.md](mecha-and-megafauna.md) in addition only when giant scale is actually part of the source or proposal.

## Evidence and story boundary

Separate two authorities that answer different questions:

- `story status`: whether the current screenplay treats life, behavior, intelligence, or contact as FACT, withheld fact, or open possibility;
- `science status`: `OBSERVED`, `SUPPORTED HABITAT`, `POTENTIAL BIOSIGNATURE`, `CONSTRAINED EXTRAPOLATION`, or `EXPLICIT HYPOTHESIS`.

A screenplay may lock an organism's existence without turning its anatomy, metabolism, senses, color, scale, or ecology into scientific fact. Habitability is not life detection. Never use an Earth analogue, search image, generated image, or familiar creature silhouette as proof of an alien body plan.

## Organism-ecology card

Define only fields that affect the current beat or asset:

1. `habitat boundary`: medium, pressure, temperature, chemistry, radiation, gravity, light, flow, substrate, and time cycle;
2. `energy and ecological role`: usable gradient or story axiom, acquisition path, scarcity, trophic or material relation, abundance, and waste;
3. `organization`: centralized individual, modular colony, distributed network, membrane, biofilm, swarm, symbiosis, cyclic material system, or another source-derived topology;
4. `support and exchange`: buoyancy/load path, boundary material, gas/liquid/chemical exchange, internal or distributed transport, homeostasis, and repair;
5. `sensing and signaling`: which environmental difference can be detected, through what carrier, at what range and latency, and with what observable response;
6. `movement or growth`: propulsion, anchoring, deformation, cilia, jetting, drift, crawling, branching, accretion, phase change, colony synchronization, or another explicit process;
7. `lifecycle`: reproduction, assembly, migration, dormancy, death, recycling, succession, or inheritance only where the source needs it;
8. `scale and population`: energy-supported size, density, speed, duty cycle, territory, and independent scale evidence when relevant;
9. `environment response`: displacement, wake, substrate change, chemical trace, light absorption/emission, heat, pressure, debris, symbiont behavior, or another caused effect;
10. `story-visible evidence`: the smallest body, behavior, trace, or ecological relation the selected shot or deliverable must reveal.

Mark each field `FACT`, `DERIVATION`, `PROPOSAL`, `LOCKED`, or `not applicable`, and retain its science-status label separately.

## Topology divergence before silhouette

When body organization is open and identity matters, compare at least three functionally valid routes before choosing one. Change organization, energy path, sensing, movement/growth, and environment coupling—not merely surface texture.

Useful route families include:

- a centralized mobile organism with a source-justified front/rear or radial relation;
- a modular or colonial body whose repeated units divide exchange, sensing, movement, or reproduction;
- a distributed field, membrane, biofilm, branching network, swarm, or habitat-organism boundary with no single face or chassis.

These are starting axes, not a closed menu. Preserve rejected routes and the reason each failed the story, environment, energy, production, or originality gate.

## Sensing, light, and color

Do not assume eyes, a face, visible hearing organs, human-readable emotion, or a head-first approach. Derive perception from a real or declared carrier such as photons, polarization, pressure, flow, vibration, electric or magnetic fields, temperature, chemistry, contact, gravity, radiation, or collective state.

Bioluminescence, fluorescence, iridescence, thermal emission, pigment, translucency, and structural color require separate mechanisms and story functions. Permanent darkness does not by itself justify blue-green bioluminescence. If an organism reacts when a lamp changes state, distinguish direct photoreception from correlated heat, electrical, chemical, or schedule cues; expose the ambiguity when the source does not decide it.

## Material and body reality

- Concentrate structure at support, exchange, sensing, growth, repair, reproduction, or contact zones rather than covering the body with equal detail.
- Repeated plates, seams, ribs, fibers, pores, sacs, chambers, mineral layers, or light points need a biological or ecological function. Reject engineered panel lines and LED-like dots unless the story explicitly establishes a biological-machine continuum.
- Show regional material behavior: compression, flex, inflation, shedding, salt or mineral growth, mucus/brine, healing, dormancy, phase change, colony edge, or another applicable response.
- Do not enlarge a whale, snake, insect, reptile, human, or franchise creature and call the result alien. After deleting names, color, setting, and scale, the organism must still retain a habitat-specific organization and behavior.

## Energy, size, and activity gate

Large size, rapid movement, dense populations, continuous emission, active heating, and complex repair all spend energy. Trace the applicable energy path and visible cost. When energy availability is uncertain or sparse, keep multiple ecological amplitudes open: microbial or film-like life, slow sparse macroscopic life, localized high-flux ecosystems, or an explicit fictional energy axiom. Do not default either to microbes or megafauna.

## Narrative and production gate

- Match behavior to the selected visual contract. Discovery does not require attack; relationship does not require domestication; establishing does not require a hero organism.
- Preserve intended uncertainty. Show only the anatomy and behavior the audience is allowed to know at this beat.
- Check medium capacity: a still may show an organism's current response and one compatible trace, but cannot prove a full perception-decision-migration sequence without durable evidence.
- For photographed or hybrid routes, define which body/contact cue can be practical, puppeteered, animatronic, performed, simulated, miniature, or VFX-extended. Do not let clean full-body CG erase the photographed anchor.

## Prompt and audit contract

Before writing a model-facing prompt, state:

`story status → science status → habitat/energy boundary → selected topology → sensing/signaling → movement/growth → regional material behavior → environment response → story-visible evidence → withheld information → Earth/franchise traits to delete`

Reject or revise when any applicable condition is true:

- a habitability claim is presented as evidence that the organism exists or has a specific body;
- the body defaults to a face, bilateral limbs, whale/snake/insect silhouette, armored vehicle shell, or upright performer without derivation;
- blue/cyan glow, LED-like dots, translucency, tentacles, or decorative filaments carry alienness without a mechanism;
- scale, speed, abundance, or emission has no energy/ecology relation;
- the organism's material, sensing, movement, and environmental response contradict one another;
- the creature could be transplanted unchanged to another medium, gravity, chemistry, energy regime, or story beat;
- the prompt reveals more identity, intention, intelligence, threat, or anatomy than the source permits.

## Primary research anchors

- NASA Europa Clipper mission science; habitability objectives and explicit non-life-detection boundary: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/europa-clipper/mission-science/
- Europa Clipper mission science paper; water, bioessential elements, chemical energy, stability, and unresolved transport/mixing: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-15160-9
- Szalay et al. 2024; directly observed oxygen production is lower than previously thought and narrows habitability estimates: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-024-02206-x
- Ruesch et al. 2025; limited present-day Europan seafloor faulting and unresolved low-energy chemosynthetic pathways: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67151-3
