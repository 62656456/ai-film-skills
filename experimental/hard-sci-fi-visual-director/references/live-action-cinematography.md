# Live-action cinematography gate

Use this reference only when the selected capture route includes live-action photography or hybrid live action/VFX. The goal is a photographed story beat, place, body, or asset with coherent extension, not a clean CG concept render.

## Reality target

Make the result plausibly describable as a real camera physically situated relative to an actor, organism, object, set, vehicle, or landscape while impossible scale and distance are extended coherently. Near, distant, fixed, mobile, intimate, observational, and obstructed relations are all valid when the screenplay justifies them. Camera, bodies, materials, reflections, medium, and VFX must resemble one exposure.

## Physical production layers

Assign each applicable layer before style language:

1. **Photographed grounding anchor**: the subject, set/environment, prop, body, material, reflection, light, or empty-space evidence that makes the selected beat photographically real. A foreground object, contact, wear, and occlusion are optional and must come from the source.
2. **Principal subject layer**: the source-required actor, child, group, organism, prop, machine, or empty-space evidence at the size needed for the selected visual contract.
3. **Near evidence layer**: practical bodies, props, surfaces, routes, reflections, restraints, domestic objects, tools, or mechanisms that make the beat and world legible.
4. **Middle extension**: repeated structure, vehicle, habitat, work crews, terrain, or orbital assembly; break repetition through function, occlusion, and state.
5. **Far invisible VFX**: planetary curvature, distant ring, kilometers of infrastructure, far orbital traffic, deep crater, or extreme atmospheric horizon with naturally reduced information.

For an empty science image, use instruments, tracks, deployed panels, thermal state, moving mechanisms, or time-dependent light as the reality anchor. Do not insert a person merely to satisfy a template.

## Camera anchor

Every prompt must answer: **Where could the camera physically be?**

Possible anchors, to be chosen only when the beat requires them:

- shoulder-height inside an airlock behind a suited worker;
- fixed to a rover service deck beside a wheel and handhold;
- clamped to a non-rotating docking truss near the capture ring;
- floating only when attached to a stated free-flyer with a plausible relative frame;
- waist-height on a Titan research vessel deck behind a heated instrument mast;
- low on a crater-rim work platform, partly masked by an insulated cable reel.

State the applicable camera location, height, subject distance, tilt, focal length/field of view, and obstruction or clean view. Reject an unlocated viewpoint or impossible camera inside a hazard. A centered, frontal, symmetric, or unobstructed photographed view is valid when the source or asset/ceremony/inspection contract requires it; reject only an unsupported showroom default that erases the beat.

## Large-format logic

- Large format is not synonymous with ultra-wide, proximity, or a moderate lens. Choose distance and focal length from information order, spatial power, body relation, and production access; use large image area to preserve texture, depth, and tonal separation appropriate to that choice.
- Use long lenses when compression serves surveillance, isolation, or alignment. Do not use them merely to make an image prestigious.
- Use extreme wide lenses only when the physical blocking requires a tight interior or close machinery. Control stretched people and curved structures.
- For a person-led frame, use the distance required to read the source's emotion, gesture, relation, or physical condition; intimacy does not always require a face close-up.
- For an environment-led frame, use script-relevant scale evidence such as occupied surfaces, habitat rhythm, vehicles, bodies, biological traces, repeated units, terrain, or light travel. People are optional.

## Optics and exposure

- Derive the capture signature from the approved camera/sensor, optics, filtration, format, exposure, display intent, and dramatic function. Natural or engineered perspective, deep or shallow focus, clean digital detail or visible texture, hard or soft edges, clipped or rolled highlights, and absent or present halation are all valid when causally selected; do not bundle them into a universal prestige look.
- Let source strength, sensor/film response, exposure intent, and dramatic information order decide clipping, flare, highlight texture, and shadow visibility. Do not flatten every image into universally protected highlights or universally crushed blacks.
- Keep flare linked to one visible or just-off-frame source. Reject decorative horizontal streaks and full-frame bloom.
- Match foreground and extension in exposure, shadow direction, contrast, sharpness, chroma, reflection, and texture.
- In atmosphere, reduce contrast and fine detail through distance according to the medium.
- In vacuum, do not add haze for depth. Use focus, scale, occlusion, exposure, light falloff on surfaces, and limited star visibility.

## Motivated light

- Establish the script-derived source hierarchy: one source may dominate, or several environmental/practical emitters may share the exposure when the location and beat require it. Name each important source and its role; fire requires an allowed medium.
- Add bounce and negative fill according to nearby surfaces. Do not rim-light every edge.
- In direct sun outside atmosphere, favor a hard distant beam and decisive shadow direction.
- In a dense atmosphere such as Titan, favor weak diffuse daylight and local functional lamps.
- In interiors, light should emerge from windows, instrument panels, task lamps, ceilings, floor strips used for circulation, or equipment state. Keep decorative light secondary.
- Treat color as causal: source spectrum, medium, material reflection, power state, or narrative condition.

## Human reality

- Keep a story-bearing actor or organism large enough to read the emotion, attention, gesture, relation, or physical work the beat actually depends on.
- At distances where the source needs bodily or material detail, select only the evidence the scene causes—pores, restfulness or fatigue, condensation, pressure marks, glove compression, grooming, wear, or another condition. Do not add distress, grime, sweat, or disarray as a generic realism filter.
- Give visible people or organisms distinct source-relevant roles, postures, attention, relations, routes, states, and timing; do not convert everyone into workers.
- Make bodies, organisms, props, controls, tools, supports, restraints, fluids, and loose objects obey the selected gravity, acceleration, medium, contact, attention, relation, or stillness. Hands, tools, feet, tethers, and task gaze are required only when the beat actually contains them.
- When the screenplay establishes a populated, busy facility and the chosen depth can read background action, use at least two purposeful groups with distinct routes or tasks while the principal subject pauses. Quiet, intimate, sleeping, empty, abandoned, ceremonial, or deliberately still scenes require none unless the source says otherwise.

## Anti-CG diagnosis

When the image resembles a game render, identify one dominant cause:

- **Everything equally sharp** → create focus and medium-specific depth; remove fake vacuum haze.
- **Everything equally clean** → add selective interface wear, repair, dust, glove polish, impact pits, thermal discoloration, or moisture only where the environment permits.
- **Everything equally detailed** → concentrate detail at work zones and load/heat paths; calm large structural surfaces.
- **Actors look pasted in** → unify source direction, reflections, grain, contact, exposure, and atmospheric state.
- **Narrative frame looks like a product render** → restore the selected function-specific evidence and camera relation. An explicitly requested asset reference may remain neutral and complete when identity, function, scale, applicable interfaces/relationships, and material logic are clear.
- **Lighting looks like a game grade** → remove unmotivated edge light and universal teal-orange; restore the actual source hierarchy, material response, exposure priority, and photographic black.
- **Scale is only an adjective when scale matters to the beat** → add enough source-appropriate independent known relationships; an occupied surface is one option, not a requirement.

## Prompt assembly

Use this order:

1. ratio, shot purpose, camera anchor, height, distance, tilt, lens, and dominant composition;
2. selected function-specific visual contract and information order;
3. photographed source-required actor, organism, object, environment, or intentional-empty-space evidence;
4. only the near, middle, and far practical/in-camera/miniature/VFX layers the composition uses;
5. physics card and visible causal pairs;
6. functional engineering and material history;
7. dominant light, exposure, bounce, practicals, and color causality;
8. human and background behavior;
9. optical continuity, atmosphere, and motion;
10. scene-specific exclusions.

## Reality audit

- Can a real camera occupy the named location?
- Is at least one source-required subject, environment, material, light, reflection, performance, contact, or intentional-empty-space anchor genuinely photographable or reproducible in the selected live-action/hybrid method? A foreground object, actor, or equipment item is not mandatory.
- Is the selected story, discovery, relationship, establishing, aftermath, or asset function readable before decorative spectacle?
- Do people and tools respond to the environment?
- Does the light have a source and one consistent shadow logic?
- Are foreground and extension matched as one exposure?
- Are material wear and optical falloff selective rather than global?
- Does the frame avoid unsupported showroom symmetry, game-render sharpness, artificial HDR, plastic skin, decorative fog, random LEDs, text, logos, and watermarks? Source-driven symmetry remains valid.

Passing this audit means the prompt is structurally ready. Only a visible generated image and user judgment establish aesthetic success.
