# Script-derived style and color system

Style and color are consequences of the current story beat, world, environment, production route, material, information order, emotion, and approved continuity. This reference fixes the reasoning method, not a house look.

## Style authority

Use this order:

1. explicit user style/color locks;
2. screenplay facts, dramatic function, emotion curve, and information reveal;
3. approved project bible and anchor frames;
4. environmental physics, materials, light sources, and production constraints;
5. reversible director proposals;
6. route vocabulary below.

No route name, genre label, director/film name, common search result, or example may override the source. A selected proposal remains CANDIDATE until the user approves it; only then may it become a project continuity lock.

## Style thesis

Write one observable sentence describing the audience relation, for example:

`The viewer discovers the world from a child's hidden eye line while large living movement remains only partly knowable through layered ice.`

Then derive these dimensions:

1. `narrative posture`: who or what leads perception, what the viewer feels, and how much is withheld;
2. `camera relation`: physical/sensor viewpoint, distance, height, lens tendency, stability, obstruction, and reveal order;
3. `spatial rhythm`: density, pause, repetition, compression, openness, and direction of attention;
4. `material-information distribution`: where operational detail concentrates and where calm surfaces or negative space are needed;
5. `light/contrast behavior`: source hierarchy, shadow, bounce, black level, highlight limit, and medium-driven depth;
6. `temporal behavior`: stillness, latency, drift, pulse, mechanical cadence, biological rhythm, or abrupt transition when relevant.

A coherent thesis may be singular, transitional, or intentionally polyphonic when the screenplay contains a real change of viewpoint, institution, environment, or emotional state. Do not force exactly one route or forbid a justified mixture; name which dimensions change and why.

## Optional route vocabulary

These are diagnostic lenses, not an exhaustive menu and not prompt tokens:

| Lens | Useful when | Observable emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering witness | procedure or operation is the beat | task path, interfaces, loads, access, honest work light |
| Human pressure | body or decision carries consequence | face/hands/contact, constrained depth, readable physical strain |
| Monumental presence | occupation and scale shape emotion | known-size foreground, cropped mass, calm surfaces, repeated units |
| Causal spectacle | one paid phenomenon disrupts normal order | deformation, moving boundary, displacement, exposure response |
| Threshold failure | crossing a technical or social limit | localized state transition, response path, before/current/after evidence |
| Quiet inquiry | observation changes knowledge | controlled reveal, negative space, sensing evidence, ambiguity |
| Frontier labor | resource-limited daily operation matters | workflow, reuse, localized wear, uneven available light |
| Controlled order | protocol, access, institution, or ceremony matters | standardization, spatial hierarchy, one meaningful deviation |

Create a custom thesis when none fits. Route selection must follow the script, not precede it.

## Color responsibilities

These are responsibilities, not five mandatory hues. Merge or omit roles when the scene does not need them:

- `world base`: star, sky, atmosphere, terrain, liquid, ice, habitat ambient, or other environmental field;
- `material identity`: local color and spectral response of structure, textile, skin, tissue, coating, soil, fluid, wear, and contamination;
- `perceptual/character anchor`: what must remain readable first for story or emotion; this need not be a person or a saturated color;
- `functional state`: power, safety, alignment, heat, navigation, authorization, biological condition, or fault only when a real system carries it;
- `event or information transition`: the color/luminance change caused by discovery, movement, weather, energy, damage, ceremony, or another scripted change.

For every active role record:

`physical source → spectrum/intensity → medium interaction → material response → exposure/white balance → screen placement and salience → persistence/continuity`

Color may also come from reflectance, absorption, interference, polarization, thermal emission, fluorescence, biological emission, display light, chemical change, or institutional material coding when physically and narratively justified.

## Deriving a palette

1. Start from the beat's information and emotion order: what must appear, disappear, separate, or remain uncertain?
2. Identify all real or hypothesized emitters, reflectors, absorbers, filters, and participating media.
3. Check materials, spectral bias, intensity, direction, scatter, absorption, adaptation, exposure, and white balance.
4. Decide which color responsibilities are active and which are intentionally absent.
5. Compare at least two palette strategies for a new project identity; keep both story-equivalent.
6. Select one as CANDIDATE and record what fact or production reason supports it.
7. After user approval, record relative relationships and transition causes in the project bible.

Do not assign universal hex values or area ratios. Exact values are valid only for an approved asset, calibrated material/light reference, brand/safety standard, or user lock with known color-management conditions.

## Light hierarchy

Every important light must have a source and path. A scene may have one source or many; the number is determined by location and story. Record:

- source position, scale, spectrum, hardness, intensity, and temporal behavior;
- medium scatter, absorption, reflection, refraction, or occlusion;
- material response and skin/tissue response;
- practical fixtures or emitters visible in frame;
- exposure priority, black level, highlight behavior, flare/halation, and distance falloff;
- which source carries story information and which sources merely maintain visibility.

Reject incompatible rim lights, decorative colored fog, and global grading that claims to create a light source. Do not flatten a richly sourced location into “one dominant environmental light” when the screenplay requires layered illumination.

## Color script and continuity

For a still, record its `current light/color state` and only the transition evidence the source can honestly contain. When a threshold moment includes compatible temporal evidence, use `before-state trace → captured perceptual transition → emerging result`; for a static reference, portrait, tableau, or unchanged scene, do not invent entering/outgoing states.

For a sequence, record three to five beats only when the screenplay changes source, medium, orientation, time, power, material, biology, or perception. Preserve relative skin/tissue, material, light, and shadow relationships across shots unless a causal transition is shown.

Color can change because:

- the subject crosses a light or medium boundary;
- orientation, time, weather, or atmospheric path changes;
- power, thermal, chemical, biological, or display state changes;
- a material is exposed, damaged, wet, frozen, contaminated, repaired, or replaced;
- the camera changes adaptation, filtration, sensor mode, or exposure for a story reason;
- an approved subjective or non-photographic production route intentionally changes perception.

A grade alone is not a causal transition.

## Production-route translation

- `photographed live action/hybrid`: sources, fixtures, reflections, set materials, skin/tissue, playback screens, atmospheric medium, and VFX extension share a coherent exposure strategy;
- `miniature-led`: scale, depth, atmosphere, source size, surface response, and compositing preserve the intended perceived mass;
- `animation/illustration`: the image may stylize value, line, texture, and color, but must still preserve physical/world causality and the source beat;
- `asset/reference`: use neutral or diagnostic lighting only if the deliverable requires it; do not confuse it with the project's dramatic palette.

## Gates

Reject or revise if any applicable answer is no:

1. Can the style thesis be traced to dramatic function, information order, environment, production route, or approved lock?
2. Is the thesis expressed through observable camera, rhythm, material-information, light, contrast, and temporal choices rather than a name?
3. Are palette choices derived from source, medium, material, exposure, story role, and continuity rather than a universal grade?
4. Does each important color/light have a real or explicitly hypothesized source and path?
5. Are strong colors and unusual darkness/brightness allowed when the screenplay and physical source justify them?
6. If more than one style logic appears, is the transition or dimensional split intentional and script-derived?
7. Are exact values, route names, palette percentages, and franchise references absent from the model-facing prompt unless explicitly locked and relevant?
8. Is the current route still labeled CANDIDATE until user approval?
9. Would replacing the palette with generic gray-blue/amber or teal-orange erase important world or story evidence? If not, derive the color system more specifically.
