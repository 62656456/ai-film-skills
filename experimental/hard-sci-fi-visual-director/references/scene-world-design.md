# Script-derived scene and world design

Use this reference to turn current screenplay needs and verified constraints into a specific place, world, and spatial experience. Build only the world evidence the story requires; do not add encyclopedia lore or a generic industrial base.

## Authority and scope

Inherit the `剧本权威卡`, selected dramatic function, approved project locks, physics card, and research evidence. The screenplay governs what happens and what the audience must understand or feel. World design solves only open gaps and necessary visual consequences.

The environment routes are constraint packs, not a closed list. Build a custom or mixed profile when the script crosses real boundaries. Do not default to near-future Earth, gray metal, repair work, a busy facility, a single environmental light, or a human operator.

## World-to-scene chain

For every consequential claim derive:

`script need → physical or speculative rule → operational consequence → resource/access/institutional consequence when relevant → spatial/material/behavioral trace → camera-readable evidence`

Possible layers include:

- physical invariants: medium, gravity, pressure, temperature, light, motion, energy, heat, biology, and time;
- speculative allowance: scope, access, latency, cost, failure, side effects, and what it does not solve;
- capability and scarcity: what can be built, grown, transported, repaired, replaced, sensed, shared, hidden, or only salvaged;
- civilization and institution: ownership, care, authority, ritual, conflict, education, trade, maintenance, safety, exclusion, or commons only when the script touches them;
- operating and lived history: construction, adaptation, reuse, aging, biological growth, damage, cleaning, ceremony, and daily habits caused by the world.

Do not add a second hidden miracle to repair the first. Do not invent rationing, authoritarian control, breakdown, or social conflict just because the setting is science fiction.

## Scene function

Before designing geography, record:

- what the viewer must perceive, learn, feel, anticipate, or not yet know;
- whose objective, attention, relationship, or absence organizes the frame;
- the operational or lived function of the location;
- why this location and moment matter;
- the selected function-specific visual contract;
- the entering and outgoing story/world state, if either changes.

Apply the matching rule:

- action/decision: make action, target, change, and script-supported consequence share a readable geography;
- discovery/information: make observer, evidence, reveal order, and intended unknown share a readable geography;
- relationship/power: make distance, access, height, blockage, exchange, or territory carry the relational shift;
- establishing/atmosphere: reveal how the place operates or remains causally empty, plus a latent route or condition; no invented crisis is required;
- aftermath/transition: place trace and affected new state so the implied cause matches the source;
- asset/reference: supply functional and scale context only when the deliverable needs it; do not invent a plot.

## Spatial skeleton

Build one coherent geography before decorative detail:

- orientation and reference frame;
- medium and pressure boundaries;
- required zones and adjacency;
- routes for the relevant people, organisms, vehicles, materials, energy, heat, data, waste, sound, sight, or escape;
- access, privacy, ritual, safety, hazard, and exclusion boundaries actually used by the beat;
- landmarks, scale relationships, and occupied or biological interfaces;
- principal line of attention, reveal, movement, or blockage;
- camera/sensor access and sightlines;
- selected production footprint and extension seam.

Do not automatically include arrival, work, control, storage, habitation, service, waste, and emergency zones. Include only those that explain the current location and event. A connected small room may be more correct than a megastructure; a civilization-scale geography may be necessary when the script authorizes it.

## Civilization and lived system

Design only social systems that causally touch the beat:

- shared standards, accommodation, care, ritual, privacy, family, education, labor, governance, trade, conflict, or public/restricted space;
- scarcity, abundance, reuse, queueing, repair, inheritance, disposal, insurance, or protected volume;
- authorization, handoff, inspection, quarantine, redundancy, responsibility, or tolerated risk;
- automation, skill boundaries, latency, local/remote presence, and tool or knowledge custody;
- logistics, food, habitat, transport, cleaning, replacement, waste return, and bottlenecks.

Translate selected systems into layout, objects, material condition, behavior, timing, and access. Avoid lore signage, costume stereotypes, and exposition posing as set dressing.

## Use and material history

History may be industrial, domestic, civic, ceremonial, biological, military, scientific, or ecological. Trace only relevant causes:

`original function → adaptation/ownership/growth → recurring stress or habit → repair/replacement/ritual/biological response → current visible state`

Distribute evidence by contact, load, heat, fluid, light, radiation, cleaning, growth, handling, weather, or social use. Do not use universal rust, grime, scratches, hanging cables, or “dirty future” aging.

## People, organisms, and active emptiness

For every visible actor group needed by the source record:

`role or relation → immediate objective/attention → position or origin → destination or blocked route → contact/gesture → environmental constraint → visible effect`

Use at least two purposeful background groups only when the screenplay establishes a populated, busy location and the shot scale can read them. For intimate, quiet, secretive, ceremonial, empty, or reference scenes, protect the intended stillness and do not add a crowd.

An empty scene can be causally active through moving shadow, thermal state, weather, tides, tracks, instrument motion, growth, traffic traces, automated operation, residue, or absence. It need not contain a malfunction.

## Production translation

For photographed/hybrid routes, give actors or practical subjects credible contact surfaces, occlusion, reflection, scale, and light; place the extension seam beyond the decisive contact when possible. For animation, illustration, miniature, or asset-reference routes, preserve the same spatial and causal logic while using that medium's actual evidence.

Move only deliverable-visible facts into the final prompt. Keep off-view rules in the bible for continuity.

## Required output

### `场景圣经`

- source/version/beat and approval status;
- narrative/lived and operational function;
- world rules, explicit hypotheses, and relevant costs;
- selected civilization/institution/economy evidence;
- use/material history;
- environment profile and sensory conditions;
- fixed/variable geography and continuity locks;
- `claim → visible evidence → readable shot scale` register;
- selected production boundary.

### `空间骨架`

- orientation and boundaries;
- required zones and adjacency;
- relevant flows, routes, blockage, and reveal order;
- hazards/access/privacy only where used;
- viewer, camera, sensor, or projection anchor; sightline, occlusion, scale proof; and production footprint, layer boundary, or extension seam only where the selected medium uses them.

## Gates

Reject or revise if any applicable answer is no:

1. Does every important world decision trace to a script need, verified constraint, approved lock, or labeled proposal?
2. Does the scene preserve the selected dramatic function and information order?
3. Do zones, routes, boundaries, and camera access form one connected place?
4. Does every included physical, social, institutional, or historical claim leave visible evidence?
5. Has the design avoided adding crisis, scarcity, maintenance, crowds, authority, or damage without a source need?
6. Do material history and activity follow specific forces, bodies, habits, and institutions rather than a generic future texture?
7. Does the selected production route have a credible contact and extension strategy at the requested scale?
8. If the location noun is replaced with `generic sci-fi base`, does important story or world logic break? If not, derive the place again.
9. Does the function-specific visual contract pass without relying on text or UI?
10. Are unapproved world choices still marked provisional or candidate?
