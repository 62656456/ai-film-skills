# Script-to-visual derivation

Load this reference before designing a project world, scene, shot, style, palette, environment, asset family, or interface from a screenplay, story passage, scene brief, approved project bible, or user premise. It owns source authority and prevents visual invention from silently rewriting the story.

## 1. Authority order

Use one current source state and this order:

1. current explicit user locks and corrections;
2. the named current screenplay/version and selected scene or beat;
3. approved project bible, continuity locks, and approved anchor frames;
4. verified research constraints relevant to an open design gap;
5. reversible director proposals;
6. optional route vocabulary and examples.

Lower levels may clarify or visualize higher levels; they may not overwrite them. A generated image, search result, existing genre convention, or Skill example never becomes project authority by itself.

If two higher-level sources conflict, stop the affected decision and expose the conflict. Do not average them. If research conflicts with a screenplay fact, offer two labeled paths: revise the story fact, or preserve it as an explicit fictional axiom and propagate its costs and limits. The user chooses.

## 2. Source and gap classification

Build `剧本权威卡` before research or visual styling:

- `source_id/version`: exact screenplay, scene, beat, project bible, or user premise used;
- `explicit facts`: time, place, people, objects, actions, dialogue-dependent information, relationships, outcomes, and stated world rules;
- `must preserve`: causal order, character intention, emotional turn, information reveal, geography, and approved continuity;
- `forbidden rewrite`: events, motivations, outcomes, identities, or project locks the image may not change;
- `necessary inference`: the smallest consequence required to make an explicit fact visible or physically coherent;
- `open design gaps`: facts the source does not decide and visual design genuinely must solve;
- `unknown that matters`: one missing answer that could materially change story meaning, cost, or continuity.

Tag every design statement:

- `FACT`: explicit in the current authority source;
- `DERIVATION`: a necessary or strongly supported consequence of a fact or verified constraint;
- `PROPOSAL`: one reversible creative choice for an open gap;
- `LOCKED`: a FACT or approved proposal that must continue within the declared scope;
- `REJECTED`: considered and explicitly not adopted.

Do not call a plausible guess a fact. When a screenplay is sparse, keep the package sparse and label proposals. Ask at most one essential question when proceeding would otherwise create an irreversible story or project decision; otherwise provide distinct candidates without pretending one is canon.

## 3. First-principles dramatic analysis

Before asking how the world looks, determine what the audience must experience in this beat:

- `dramatic function`: action/decision, discovery/information, relationship/power, establishing/atmosphere, aftermath/transition, or isolated asset/reference;
- `character objective`: what a person, group, organism, or responsible system is trying to obtain, avoid, conceal, learn, preserve, or change;
- `obstacle or pressure`: physical, social, institutional, temporal, environmental, informational, or internal;
- `emotion curve`: entering feeling → pressure/change → leaving feeling;
- `information order`: what the viewer knows first, what becomes legible next, and what must remain withheld;
- `spatial power`: who controls distance, access, height, visibility, routes, resources, or attention;
- `required evidence`: what must be visible for the beat to work without explanatory lore.

The screenplay governs dramatic facts and functions. It rarely determines one inevitable silhouette, palette, lens, or lighting scheme. Research and directing fill only the open gaps, and multiple proposals may be equally valid.

## 4. Function-specific visual contracts

Choose the contract that matches the beat. Do not force an action-scene gate onto every image.

### Action or decision

Make `actor or responsible system → action → physical target → changed state → consequence or cost` readable. A beneficiary is required only when the script contains one. A persistent cost is required only when the beat contains or implies one.

### Discovery or information

Make `observer → physical evidence → change in attention or understanding` readable. Preserve intentionally withheld information. Awe, ambiguity, secrecy, or uncertainty may be the correct outcome; do not invent a breakdown, rescue, alarm, or sacrifice to create drama.

### Relationship or power

Make `spatial control, access, distance, gesture, object exchange, or blockage → response → relational or emotional shift` readable. Technology and setting support the relationship rather than replacing it with an engineering procedure.

### Establishing or atmosphere

Make `location function → active world or causally active emptiness → latent route, condition, or pressure` readable. No arbitrary emergency or protagonist is required. The shot succeeds when the viewer understands how this place lives and what kind of story can occur here.

### Aftermath or transition

Make `material, bodily, spatial, institutional, or environmental trace → implied cause → affected new state` readable. The cause may remain offscreen when the script withholds it.

### Isolated asset or reference

Show `identity → function → scale → applicable interfaces or relationships → material/operating logic`. A passive material, natural object, organism, or interface-free asset need not invent a connector or user. Do not invent a user, crisis, opponent, damage, or story event merely to make an asset sheet dramatic.

For every contract, UI text, captions, and lore may confirm but cannot carry the only essential evidence. The correct reading time and ambiguity follow the screenplay: some action beats should read in three seconds; some discoveries should unfold; some suspense beats must withhold the answer.

## 4A. Deliverable-capacity gate

Before composing, test whether the requested medium can carry the script's information order:

- A single still can hold simultaneous relations and one threshold moment. Use a physically compatible `before-state trace → current action/state → emerging result` only when those states can coexist at one instant.
- Do not compress mutually exclusive states—lamp fully on and already off, hatch sealed and open, intact and fully destroyed, unread and already understood—into one frame merely to explain the plot.
- If a prior action has no durable trace or the consequence cannot yet be visible, state what the still can and cannot prove. Offer a two-frame pair, storyboard beat, short motion shot, or another medium when allowed.
- An interface, caption, symbolic glow, or extra character may not be added solely to exceed the medium's information capacity.
- An isolated asset sheet or UI study uses view/state coverage rather than fictional time; declare which views or states are alternatives, not consecutive story moments.

Failure to fit the whole beat into one still is not permission to rewrite the screenplay. Preserve the source, choose the strongest honest threshold, and expose the remaining ambiguity for user judgment.

## 5. Script-to-design derivation chain

For each consequential visual choice, record:

`script need → operational need → physical, environmental, biological, institutional, or production constraint → design response → visible evidence → deliverable- or viewer-appropriate evidence`

Examples of valid questions:

- What must be close, blocked, exposed, rationed, shared, hidden, or hard to reach for this beat to occur?
- What does the character notice first, and therefore what may the camera reveal first?
- Which environmental property changes movement, body behavior, sound, light, material, or access?
- Which object or spatial boundary carries the relationship or decision?
- What must remain practical for performance, and what may extend beyond the set?

Reject a design decision that has no upstream script need or approved project reason. Also reject the reverse error: do not shrink a script-authorized wonder merely because the nearest real prototype is modest.

## 6. Deriving the project visual system

World, environment, style, color, and camera are separate decisions. Derive them in this order, while allowing iteration:

1. `world`: physical rules, speculative allowances, capability, scarcity, institutions, daily behavior, and history required by the screenplay;
2. `environment`: medium, gravity, pressure, climate, geography, architecture, hazards, routes, and sensory conditions that enable the scene;
3. `production route`: photographed live action, hybrid live action/VFX, miniature-led, animation, illustration, asset reference, or another user-locked form;
4. `style thesis`: the observable audience relation created by narrative posture, camera relation, material-information distribution, rhythm, and contrast;
5. `color system`: sources, materials, exposure, functional state, character separation, information order, emotional transition, and continuity;
6. `camera`: viewpoint, lens, distance, height, occlusion, movement, focus, and reveal order that best serves the beat.

The environment routes and style routes in other references are diagnostic vocabulary and constraint packs, not closed menus. A custom or mixed route is valid when the screenplay crosses real boundaries. Do not select an environment, style, palette, or lens because it is the Skill default or because it is common in search results.

## 7. Approval and continuity states

Use these states precisely:

- `OPEN`: the source leaves the decision unresolved;
- `PROVISIONAL`: one working assumption is used for this exploration only;
- `CANDIDATE`: a deliberate option is ready for review;
- `APPROVED`: the user approved it within a named scope;
- `LOCKED`: approval has become a continuity requirement for the named project/version;
- `REJECTED`: explicitly removed from the active route.

Only `APPROVED` or `LOCKED` choices may enter the project bible as authority. A strong internal audit, generated file, or selected favorite by the model remains `CANDIDATE` until the user accepts it.

When a project lock changes, record the reason, version, affected downstream assets, and whether old candidates remain valid. Per-shot camera, weather, local state, and accent may vary when the project bible permits them and the screenplay supplies a cause.

## 8. Required cards

### `剧本权威卡`

Source/version/beat; FACT; must preserve; forbidden rewrite; necessary inference; open gaps; one material unknown.

### `剧本—视觉转译卡`

Dramatic function; objective/obstacle; emotion curve; information order; spatial power; selected function contract; each major `script need → design response → visible and deliverable- or viewer-appropriate evidence` chain.

### `视觉决策状态表`

For world, environment, production route, style, color, camera, asset family, and interface: decision; FACT/DERIVATION/PROPOSAL; OPEN/PROVISIONAL/CANDIDATE/APPROVED/LOCKED/REJECTED; authority/evidence; scope.

## 9. Gates

Reject or revise if any applicable answer is no:

1. Is one current source/version identified?
2. Are explicit facts, necessary inferences, open gaps, proposals, and prohibited rewrites separated?
3. Does the selected visual contract match the beat's actual dramatic function?
4. Does every major visual choice trace back to a script need, verified constraint, or approved project lock?
5. Did research answer open gaps rather than silently rewrite the screenplay?
6. Are world, environment, style, color, and camera derived independently enough to avoid a bundled house style?
7. Are unapproved choices still labeled provisional or candidate?
8. If the scene is quiet, relational, establishing, ambiguous, or reference-only, has the design avoided inventing crisis, maintenance, sacrifice, UI, or an adult technician?
9. If the scene is action or decision, is its causal change readable without relying on UI text?
10. Does the final prompt preserve the intended information order and emotional turn?
